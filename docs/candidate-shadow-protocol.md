# Frozen candidate shadow protocol

Written before inspecting individual holdout outcomes on 27 November 2026. A feasibility query had already exposed the aggregate holdout size and stored mover count (26 episodes, 14 movers), so the holdout was not fully unseen. This is an offline feasibility analysis. No production settings or alert behavior change.

Use the 26 November snapshot at cutoff 2026-11-26T19:47:11.507850Z. The development evidence is the 23 November snapshot and the prior candidate-timing report, which found many pre-crossing scores below 45. Do not optimize on the holdout. The holdout consists of fixed-anchor episodes from 2026-11-24T19:54:18.610593Z (24-hour embargo after the earlier snapshot cutoff) through 2026-11-25T19:47:11.507850Z inclusive, so the whole 24-hour outcome horizon is mature at cutoff. Report overlap and repeated assets; these records are not independent and are an event-conditioned cohort, not the universe of all asset-days.

Compare recorded V1.1 numeric candidates to one exploratory shadow: the first V1.1 evaluation with pre_score >= 35 and an existing `below_threshold` rejection or recorded `eligible` flag. The 35 threshold is an illustrative round-number decrease from 45, chosen from the earlier diagnosis, not optimized or a deployment recommendation. Excluded, failed, cooldown and budget-suppressed rows are not promoted solely by score. For every shadow evaluation require detection, evidence cutoff, market timestamp, scan start and completion at or before its effective decision time (scan completion) and before the crossing. Reject missing timestamps, post-completion inputs and decisions outside anchor-to-outcome window. Strictly earlier than crossing means early; equality is not early. Compare the recorded candidate timestamp to the same strict crossing rule, and report its weaker timestamp provenance separately. This is not an exact replay of V1.1 eligibility.

Define a mover as the first sampled price strictly after anchor, at most 24 hours after anchor, at least 105% of positive anchor price, within snapshot cutoff. A no-crossing episode is eligible as a control only if it has observations near both ends (within 15 minutes) and no consecutive gap over 15 minutes across the window; otherwise label outcome unknown. Sampling cannot prove a move did not happen between samples. Count candidate-positive episodes and their early mover recall, no-crossing control rate, lead times, missing evaluation coverage, and overlap; avoid claims of population-level precision or false alerts per asset-day. Retrospective episode selection, imperfect candidate timestamp provenance, missing observations and inability to reconstruct unchanged decision states limit conclusions. The protocol reports infeasibility instead of replacing missing evidence with zeros.
